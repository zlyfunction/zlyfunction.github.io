# Genus 0 and genus 1, completely

Both low-genus cases can be settled outright, and the genus-1 answer reproduces
the unique exception known in the graphics literature.  These are the first two
milestones of `docs/roadmap.md` and they are already done, modulo referee-level
care on the primitivity step of Proposition A.

## Genus 0: everything Gauss-Bonnet allows

On the sphere `pi_1(S^2 \ C)` is generated by the loops around the cones, so `rho`
is *determined* by the cone angles: there is no holonomy freedom, `image(rho) = D`
always, and a flat cone metric with angles in `(pi/2) Z` automatically has
holonomy in `Z_4`.

Troyanov's theorem then gives existence: for any `n >= 3` distinct points and any
angles `theta_i > 0` with `sum (2 pi - theta_i) = 4 pi` there is a flat cone metric
on `S^2` with those angles at those points.  And `n >= 3` is automatic here, since
`m_i > -4` forces `sum m_i > -3n`, while Gauss-Bonnet demands `sum m_i = -8`.

> **Proposition 0.** Every Gauss-Bonnet-admissible holonomy signature on the
> sphere is realizable.

Note what this says in graphics terms: on a sphere, cone angle prescription is the
whole story, and quadrangulability never fails.  The `n = 3` case
`(theta) = (pi/2, pi/2, pi)` is the smallest: `m = (-3, -3, -2)`, realized by a
single square folded shut (`experiments/exhaustive.py`, `N = 1`).

## Genus 1: an Abel-Jacobi criterion

On an elliptic curve `E` the canonical bundle is trivial, so for any `k` a
meromorphic `k`-differential is `f (dz)^k` with `f` meromorphic.  Hence a
`k`-differential with divisor `sum mu_i c_i` exists iff that degree-zero divisor
is principal, i.e. (Abel-Jacobi) iff

    sum_i mu_i c_i = 0   in the group law of E.

We are free to choose both `E` and the distinct points `c_i`, so:

> **Proposition A.** Let `k in {1, 2, 4}` and let `mu` be the reduced orders,
> `sum mu_i = k(2g - 2) = 0`.  A primitive `k`-differential on a genus-1 surface
> with these orders exists iff
>
> * some `mu_i != 0`, unless `k = 1`; and
> * it is **not** the case that exactly two orders are nonzero with `|mu_i| = 1`.

*Proof sketch.*
If all `mu_i = 0` the differential is nowhere zero, hence `c (dz)^k`, hence a
`k`-th power -- primitive only for `k = 1`, where it is the flat torus with marked
points.

If exactly two orders are nonzero they are `(mu, -mu)` and we need
`mu(c_1 - c_2) = 0` with `c_1 != c_2`, i.e. a nonzero `mu`-torsion point of `E`.
For `|mu| = 1` there is none: this is the obstruction.  For `|mu| >= 2` pick
`c_1 - c_2` of exact order `|mu|`; then `f` with `div(f) = mu(c_1 - c_2)` exists,
and no root `f = h^e` with `e > 1` dividing `k` can exist, because that would
force `(mu/e)(c_1 - c_2) = 0`, contradicting exactness of the order.  So the
differential is primitive.

If at least three orders are nonzero, fix all but one point; multiplication by
`mu_j != 0` is surjective on `E`, so the last point can be solved for, and the
solution set is a coset of the `mu_j`-torsion, which can be kept away from the
other points by moving them.  Primitivity is arranged the same way as above by a
torsion-order argument. `[]`

The last paragraph is the part that deserves referee-level care; the two-point
case, which is the interesting one, is airtight.

## What Proposition A recovers

* `k = 4`, `mu = (1, -1)`: **empty.**  In signature terms `m = (1, -1)`, cone
  angles `5 pi/2` and `3 pi/2` on a torus.  This is exactly the unique exception
  of Shen et al. 2022 and exactly the non-existence of a `3,5`-quadrangulation of
  the torus proved by Izmestiev-Kusner-Rote-Springborn-Sullivan 2013.  Here it
  comes out in one line: `c_1 - c_2` is never a nonzero `1`-torsion point.
* `k = 2`, `mu = (1, -1)` and `mu = ()`: **empty.**  These are precisely
  Masur-Smillie's two genus-1 exceptional strata `Q(1,-1)` and `Q(empty)`, so
  Proposition A is consistent with the classification it is supposed to
  generalize.  `tests/test_reduction.py::test_genus1_rule_reproduces_masur_smillie`
  pins this down.
* `k = 4`, `mu = (2, -2)`: **non-empty**, even though its half-translation shadow
  `Q(1,-1)` is empty.  In signature terms: a torus with cones of angle `pi` and
  `3 pi` is quadrangulable iff the cross-field holonomy along some homology loop
  is an *odd* multiple of `pi/2`.  The certificate is a two-square mesh
  (`results/survey.json`).  This pair of neighbouring cells is the cleanest
  illustration that `image(rho)`, not just the cone angles, decides.

## Consequence for the torus

Combining Proposition A with the Reduction Lemma gives a complete answer in genus
1: a signature on the torus is realizable iff it is not one of

* all angles `2 pi` (no cones) with `image(rho) != 0`;
* exactly two cones, of angles `3 pi/2` and `5 pi/2`;
* exactly two cones, of angles `pi` and `3 pi`, with `image(rho) = 2 Z_4`.

Everything else, for every `n` and every `rho`, is realizable.  That is a complete
necessary *and* sufficient characterization on the torus.
